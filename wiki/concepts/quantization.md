## Definition
Quantization maps continuous floating-point weights and/or activations to a lower-precision discrete format (e.g., INT8, INT4, 1-bit). This reduces memory footprint, enables faster integer arithmetic, and cuts energy consumption — with some accuracy cost depending on the precision floor and method.

**Unified taxonomy** (Berkeley survey [arxiv:2103.13630]):
```
Q(r) = Int(r/S) - Z,    S = (β-α)/(2^b - 1)
```
Design dimensions: uniformity (uniform/codebook), symmetry (symmetric Z=0 / asymmetric Z≠0), calibration (static/dynamic), granularity (layerwise/channelwise/sub-channelwise), fine-tuning method (PTQ/QAT), bit-width (INT8/INT4/mixed/binary). See 2103.13630.

## Major Variants

- **Sensitivity-driven binning + gradient-weighted clustering**: **Bin & Quant / GWK** [arxiv:2210.05111] (Sakthi, Yadla, Pawate — UT Austin / Cadence, 2022). Three-tier pipeline: (1) **B&Q** (no retraining, float models) — uses layer sensitivity analysis (Gaussian perturbation, accuracy drop) to drive adaptive binning; inverse CDF of Gaussian places more bins at distribution center; SPC 13.66× compression, VGG-16 4.56×. (2) **B&Q for INT8 quantized models** — applies additional storage compression on top of PTQ INT8 without retraining; MobileNetV2 1.58× extra compression at 66.91% accuracy. (3) **Gradient Weighted K-means (GWK)** (with retraining) — centroid c_j = Σᵢ gᵢwᵢ / Σᵢ gᵢ, gradient-weighted mean biases cluster centers toward sensitive weights; ResNet-20 35× compression at 90.48% on CIFAR-10 (sub-1-bit at 0.904 bits/weight). Key insight: sensitivity is not correlated with layer size — the most parameter-dense layer is often not the most sensitive. For HaLViT: when W and Wᵀ are tied, the GWK centroid must integrate gradients from both forward paths (∂L/∂W from W→x path + ∂L/∂W from Wᵀ→x' path) — the composite gradient changes the centroid in a non-obvious way, raising the question of whether gradient-weighted clustering remains valid for shared weights. Tested on CNNs and speech models only; no transformer benchmarks. See 2210.05111.
- **Trained quantization (k-means / codebook)**: **Deep Compression** [arxiv:1510.00149] (Han, Mao, Dally, Stanford/NVIDIA, ICLR 2016 Best Paper) introduced k-means trained quantization as stage 2 of a three-stage pipeline (prune → k-means quantize → Huffman code). Cluster weights per layer into k centroids; each weight stores only log₂(k)-bit cluster index; retrain centroids with accumulated gradients: ∂L/∂C_k = Σ_{I_{ij}=k} ∂L/∂W_{ij}. Linear centroid initialization best. AlexNet: 8-bit CONV (256 centroids) + 5-bit FC (32 centroids) = no accuracy loss. Combined with pruning: AlexNet 35×, VGG-16 49×, no accuracy loss (Table 1). Scales to ImageNet CNNs. Requires retraining — GPTQ/AWQ replace this with one-shot PTQ to scale to LLMs. See 1510.00149.
- **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ)**: Quantize a pretrained model without fine-tuning. Fast to apply; GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant are PTQ methods. Now achieves near-lossless at 4-bit for LLMs. **GPTQ** [arxiv:2210.17323] is the landmark second-order PTQ method: extends Optimal Brain Quantization with arbitrary-order insight + lazy batch updates + Cholesky reformulation. Quantizes OPT-175B in 4.2h on one A100; 4-bit PPL 8.37 vs FP16 8.34 (+0.03); 3-bit PPL 8.64 while RTN collapses to 7300. Calibration: 128 C4 samples, no retraining. Custom CUDA kernel achieves 3.24× throughput on A100. Hessian H = 2XX^T is per-layer and single-path — see open questions for shared-weight implications. See [[sources/2210.17323]].
- **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT)**: Simulate quantization during training using straight-through estimator (STE). More accurate than PTQ but requires retraining. **Jacob et al. 2018** [arxiv:1712.05877] (TensorFlow Lite) is the canonical INT8 QAT paper: affine quantization scheme `r = S·(q−Z)` maps weights and activations to INT8 with INT32 bias; fake quant nodes are injected during training so FP32 gradients flow through (STE) while the forward pass mimics INT8 rounding. Batch normalization is folded into weights before quantizing. ReLU6 outperforms ReLU because bounding activations to [0,6] provides a natural fixed range. Results: ~2× latency reduction on Qualcomm Snapdragon 835 ARM CPUs for MobileNets at only ~1-2% accuracy drop; ResNets within 2% of FP32. Adopted in TensorFlow Lite. For HaLViT: QAT fake quant nodes applied to shared weight W must combine STE gradients from both the W→x and Wᵀ→x' forward paths — straightforward but must be implemented explicitly in a custom BitLinear-style module. See [[sources/1712.05877]].
- **Activation-aware PTQ**: AWQ and OmniQuant identify salient weights via activation magnitude; protecting ~1% of weights dramatically reduces quantization error. **AWQ** [arxiv:2306.00978] (MLSys 2024 Best Paper) is the landmark method: saliency is determined by *activation magnitude* (not weight magnitude) — channels with high activation magnitude process salient features. Rather than keeping them in FP16, AWQ scales salient channels up by s > 1 before quantization (reducing their quantization error by 1/s), then absorbs s^{-1} into the preceding operator. Scale search: s = s_X^α, s_X = per-channel mean activation magnitude from calibration, α ∈ [0,1] grid-searched with no backprop. Only 16 calibration sequences (10× fewer than GPTQ); domain-robust (+0.5-0.6 PPL shift vs GPTQ's +2.3-4.9). LLaMA-7B INT4: 5.78 (AWQ) vs 6.22 (GPTQ); INT3: 6.35 vs 8.81. AWQ+GPTQ are orthogonal — combining gives best INT2. TinyChat system: 3.9× speedup vs HuggingFace FP16 on RTX 4090. See [[sources/2306.00978]].
- **Group-wise PTQ with layer-by-layer KD**: **ZeroQuant** [arxiv:2206.01861] (Yao et al., Microsoft, NeurIPS 2022) is an end-to-end PTQ pipeline targeting billion-scale Transformers. Two granularity innovations: (1) **group-wise weight quantization** — divide weight matrix into groups of g consecutive elements (e.g., g=128), one scale per group; finer than layerwise, coarser than channelwise, hardware-compatible; (2) **token-wise activation quantization** — per-token scale computed dynamically, addressing the large activation range variance across layers (up to 50× in GPT-3). For INT4 weights, adds **Layer-by-layer KD (LKD)**: distill layer-by-layer using only a calibration dataset (not original training data); only one extra layer in GPU memory at a time; 33s for BERT-base. Results: INT8 cost-free: 5.19×/4.16× speedup on BERT-base/GPT-3-350M; INT4/INT8 mixed + LKD: 3× memory reduction; GPT-NeoX-20B: 2 GPUs→1 GPU, 65ms→25ms. For HaLViT: group-wise quantization avoids the per-channel ambiguity for shared W (input channels of W = output channels of Wᵀ). See 2206.01861.
- **Mixed-precision decomposition for outlier features**: **LLM.int8()** [arxiv:2208.07339] (Dettmers, Lewis, Belkada, Zettlemoyer — UW/FAIR/HuggingFace, NeurIPS 2022) solves the scale-dependent quantization collapse in LLMs. Key discovery: at 6.7B+ parameters a **phase shift** causes all transformer layers to develop systematic outlier features — extreme magnitude values (~20× larger) concentrated in only ~6 feature dimensions, but covering 150k outliers per sequence. Setting these 0.1% of features to zero degrades perplexity by 600–1000%. Two-part method: (1) **vector-wise quantization** — separate normalization constant per row/column of each inner product (works to 2.7B); (2) **mixed-precision decomposition** — isolate outlier feature dimensions into FP16 matmul; quantize the remaining 99.9% to INT8. Result: zero performance degradation on OPT-175B and BLOOM-176B; enables loading on a single consumer GPU server. For HaLViT: at 11M–22M params, HaLViT is far below the 6.7B outlier threshold — standard INT8 (layerwise or channelwise) should suffice without mixed-precision. See 2208.07339.
- **Learnable PTQ (bridging QAT/PTQ)**: **OmniQuant** [arxiv:2308.13137] (Shao, Chen, Luo et al. — Shanghai AI Lab/HKU, ICLR 2024) freezes base weights and learns only quantization parameters via SGD + block-wise error minimization: (1) **LWC** — per-channel weight clipping thresholds optimized via gradient (vs hand-crafted in GPTQ/AWQ); (2) **LET** — learnable equivalent transformation that shifts quantization difficulty from activations to weights (learnable SmoothQuant). LLaMA-7B W4A4: 52.65% accuracy vs SmoothQuant 38.41% and LLM-QAT 46.43%, at 128 samples / 1.6h / 1 A100-40G (vs 100K samples / 90h for LLM-QAT). For HaLViT shared W: LWC's threshold jointly optimized over both W→x and Wᵀ→x' passes; LET's transformation constrained to simultaneously satisfy both pathways — enforced differentiably rather than analytically (may converge to a valid joint solution automatically). See [[sources/2308.13137]].
- **Incoherence-based 2-bit**: **QuIP** [arxiv:2307.13304] (Chee, Cai, Kuleshov, De Sa — Cornell, NeurIPS 2023) achieves the first viable 2-bit LLM quantization by enforcing *incoherence* — pre/post-multiplying W and H by Kronecker products of random orthogonal matrices before adaptive rounding (LDLQ). Incoherence makes weights even in magnitude and rounding directions unaligned with coordinate axes, which is mathematically equivalent to the outlier suppression performed heuristically by SmoothQuant, SpQR, and LLM.int8(). LDLQ without incoherence processing = GPTQ. At >2B params, 2-bit vs 4-bit gap is small and shrinks with model size. Provides first theoretical analysis for LLM-scale PTQ. For HaLViT shared W: orthogonal pre/post-processing must be symmetric — whether a single rotation simultaneously incoherences both W→x and Wᵀ→x' contexts is an open theoretical question. See [[sources/2307.13304]].
- **Mixed-precision**: Apply different bit widths to different layers. Sensitive layers keep FP16; compressible layers use INT4.
- **Extreme low-bit (1–2 bit)**: BitNet and its 1.58-bit variant use ternary {-1, 0, 1} weights trained from scratch, matching FP16 perplexity. QuIP achieves 2-bit with incoherence processing guarantees. **BitNet b1.58** [arxiv:2402.17764] is the landmark ternary-weight method: absmean quantization W_tilde = RoundClip(W/γ+ε, -1, 1) with γ = mean(|W_ij|); 8-bit per-token activations. Replaces nn.Linear with BitLinear; requires training from scratch via STE. At 3B+ parameters matches FP16 LLaMA PPL while using 3.55× less memory and achieving 2.71× lower latency. 70B variant: 8.9× throughput, 11× batch size, 71.4× less matmul energy (7nm, addition-only). PTQ (GPTQ, SparseGPT) cannot be applied post-hoc — BitNet b1.58 is a distinct model class. See 2402.17764.
- **Offline activation smoothing (W8A8)**: **SmoothQuant** [arxiv:2211.10438] (Xiao, Lin, Han et al., MIT/NVIDIA, ICML 2023) solves the W8A8 challenge by migrating quantization difficulty from activations to weights offline. For Y=XW, introduce scale s per input channel: X̂ = X·diag(s)⁻¹, Ŵ = diag(s)·W — mathematically equivalent but both easy to quantize. Scale: s_j = max|X_j|^α / max|W_j|^{1-α}, α tuned on calibration data. Ŵ is computed once before deployment; all runtime matrix multiplications are pure INT8. Results: 1.56× speedup, 2× memory on FasterTransformer; OPT-175B with half the GPUs; 530B on one 8-GPU node; negligible accuracy loss. Hardware-efficient contrast to LLM.int8() (mixed FP16/INT8 per op). For HaLViT: shared W needs joint calibration across both W→x and Wᵀ→x' paths since diag(s)·W and W·diag(s') cannot simultaneously satisfy both scales unless s = s'. See 2211.10438.
- **Sparse quantization / Dense-and-Sparse**: Two concurrent frameworks independently validate this paradigm. **SpQR** [arxiv:2306.03078] (Dettmers, Frantar, Alistarh et al., UW/IST/ETH, 2023): identify outlier weights → store in FP16 → compress bulk to 3-4 bit; custom GPU decoder achieves 4× compression + 15% speedup vs FP16; <1% PPL on LLaMA 7B–65B. **SqueezeLLM** [arxiv:2306.07629] (Kim, Hooper, Gholami, Keutzer et al., UC Berkeley, ICML 2024): adds Hessian-guided non-uniform bin placement to the same D&S decomposition — bins cluster near sensitive values (second-order insight beyond GPTQ's sequential updates); LLaMA-7B 3-bit: 2.1× better PPL gap vs GPTQ at same memory; 2.3× speedup on A6000. Key frame: LLM inference is memory-bandwidth-bound, not compute-bound, for single-batch generation. Both methods extend LLM.int8()'s outlier insight from 8-bit to 3-4 bit. For HaLViT scale (11M–22M): standard uniform PTQ suffices; these methods define the near-lossless 3-4 bit frontier for larger models. See [[sources/2306.03078]], [[sources/2306.07629]].

## Trade-offs

| Bit-width | Memory saving | Accuracy loss | Requires retraining | Method family |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|---------------------|---------------|
| INT8 | ~4× | Minimal | No (PTQ) or Yes (QAT) | Jacob 2018, GPTQ |
| INT4 | ~8× | Small | No (PTQ) | AWQ, GPTQ |
| 2-bit | ~16× | Moderate | No (QuIP) | QuIP |
| 1-bit (ternary) | ~32× | Minimal if trained from scratch | Yes | BitNet b1.58 |

QAT vs. PTQ: QAT (Jacob 2018) achieves higher accuracy at same bit-width but requires a full training run. PTQ (GPTQ, AWQ) needs only a calibration set and is much faster to apply — critical for LLMs where retraining is expensive. For HaLViT, PTQ is the practical first-pass; QAT is the higher-accuracy but costlier option.

## Open Questions

- **Joint SmoothQuant for HaLViT (thesis experiment)**: SmoothQuant's offline scale migration `Ŵ = diag(s)·W` is valid for single-use W, but HaLViT uses W in both W→x (scales rows) and Wᵀ→x' (scales columns) paths. A single scale cannot simultaneously optimize both unless s = s' (unlikely — activation statistics of the two paths differ). Joint calibration must find s minimizing quantization error across both forward paths simultaneously. See 2211.10438.
- **QAT + HaLViT shared weights (thesis experiment)**: Jacob et al.'s QAT injects fake quant nodes on W; STE gradient flows back to W from a single forward path. For HaLViT, both W and Wᵀ are used in the same forward pass — the STE must accumulate gradients from both paths before updating the single shared parameter. This is implementable with a custom module but must be done explicitly. See [[sources/1712.05877]].

- **HaLViT + Quantization**: HaLViT's Discussion explicitly calls for pruning + quantization composition. The key risk: quantization errors in a shared weight W propagate to all operations that use W and Wᵀ simultaneously — error amplification is the central unknown. See [[sources/halvit]].
- **GPTQ + shared weights (thesis experiment)**: GPTQ's Hessian H = 2XX^T is a single-path, per-layer construction. For HaLViT's W+Wᵀ sharing, a correct Hessian must account for BOTH the forward (Wx) and transpose (Wᵀx') pathways: H_joint = 2(X₁X₁^T + X₂X₂^T). Standard GPTQ applied naively will under-compensate the Wᵀ path — quantization quality will be worse than predicted. Extending GPTQ to handle joint-path Hessians for tied weights is an open research problem. See gptq-method.
- At what bit-width does the shared weight assumption break down — does sharing double the quantization noise budget?
- Does the activation-salient weight observation from LLMs (AWQ) transfer to ViT attention weight matrices?
- **2-bit floor for shared weights**: GPTQ achieves 2-bit on OPT-175B with only +0.11 PPL. Can 2-bit be achieved for HaLViT's shared weight matrices, or does sharing raise the effective quantization floor?
- **BitNet b1.58 + HaLViT composition (thesis experiment)**: Both are training-time modifications. In a joint training run, W+Wᵀ sharing (HaLViT) + ternary weights (BitNet b1.58) could co-exist. The critical unknown: absmean quantization computes γ = mean(|W_ij|) over the flat matrix — does the transposition symmetry HaLViT uses (treating W and Wᵀ as the same parameter) interact correctly with per-tensor absmean? If the scale γ is shared for W and Wᵀ, the quantization error for the transposed pathway may differ from the forward pathway. See bitnet-model.
- **Joint AWQ for HaLViT shared weights (thesis experiment)**: AWQ calibrates per-input-channel scales using activation statistics from the W→x forward path only. For HaLViT's W+Wᵀ, the input channels of W are the output channels of Wᵀ, and their activation magnitudes in the Wᵀ→x' path generally differ. A single-path AWQ calibration will correctly protect W→x salient channels but mis-scale Wᵀ→x' channels. Joint AWQ must measure activation statistics on BOTH paths and find per-channel scales minimizing error across both uses simultaneously — the AWQ analogue of the joint-Hessian (GPTQ) and symmetric-mask (SparseGPT) open questions. See awq-method.

## Relevance to Project

Quantization is the most immediately composable technique with HaLViT. Applying GPTQ or AWQ PTQ to HaLViT's shared weights after training could yield multiplicative compression (sharing × quantization). The risk: quantization errors in shared weights propagate to all layers using them, potentially amplifying degradation.
