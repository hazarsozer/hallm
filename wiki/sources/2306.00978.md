## Problem

Weight-only PTQ (GPTQ, RTN) applies uniform quantization across all weight channels. This ignores the empirical observation that **not all weights are equally important**: a small fraction (~0.1–1%) of "salient" weights accounts for disproportionate quantization error. Identifying these salient weights by weight magnitude (L2 norm) is ineffective — the correct signal is the *activation magnitude* of the corresponding input channel. Keeping 1% of channels in FP16 (mixed precision) nearly solves the problem but is hardware-inefficient (requires separate FP16 and INT4 compute paths). GPTQ requires 192 calibration sequences and overfits to calibration domain, causing +2.3–4.9 PPL when calibration ≠ evaluation distribution.

## Method

### Salient Weight Identification via Activation Magnitude

Weights corresponding to **input channels with larger activation magnitudes are more important**: these channels process more salient features, so errors in their weights propagate more damage to the output. This holds across model families and architectures.

**Empirical validation (Table 1, OPT-6.7B INT3-g128)**:
- RTN (baseline): PPL 23.54
- Keep 1% salient channels (by weight magnitude) in FP16: PPL 22.45 (negligible)
- Keep 1% salient channels (by activation magnitude) in FP16: PPL **10.43** (massive improvement)
- Random 1% in FP16: PPL 24.23

### Activation-Aware Scaling: The Core Idea

Instead of keeping salient channels in FP16 (mixed precision), AWQ applies an equivalent transformation: **scale up salient channels before quantization, then scale the activation down** by the same factor:

```
Q(w · s) · (x / s) ≡ Q(w) · x    (same output, different quantization grid)
```

**Quantization error analysis** (Eq. 2–3):
```
Q(w) = Δ · Round(w/Δ),  Δ = max(|w|) / (2^N - 1)

Err(Q(w)·x)   = Δ · RoundErr(w/Δ) · x
Err(Q(w·s)·x/s) = Δ' · RoundErr(ws/Δ') · x · (1/s)
```
Since scaling a single channel w usually doesn't change the group max Δ much: Δ' ≈ Δ, and the error for the salient channel is reduced by **1/s**. The non-salient channels' error increases by Δ'/Δ, creating a trade-off — optimal s balances both effects.

**Empirical optimal**: s = 2 for OPT-6.7B gives PPL 11.92 vs RTN 23.54 (Table 2). Larger s (s=4: 12.36) starts harming non-salient channels.

### Searching for Optimal Per-Channel Scale

The scale s^{-1} is absorbed into the *preceding operator* (LayerNorm scale, prior linear weight) — no mixed-precision hardware needed. Hardware sees pure INT4 weights at inference.

**Search objective** (Eq. 4–5):
```
s* = argmin_s ||Q(W · diag(s)) · (diag(s)^{-1} · X) - W·X||

s = s_X^α,   s_X = per-channel mean activation magnitude from calibration
α ∈ [0, 1],  searched by grid (20 values)
```

`s_X` is the per-(input-)channel average activation magnitude collected from a small calibration set. α=0 means no scaling (RTN); α=1 means full activation-magnitude scaling. The single scalar α balances protection of salient vs. non-salient channels.

**No backpropagation, no reconstruction** — AWQ only measures activation statistics offline (average magnitude per channel). This makes it:
- **10× more data-efficient**: 16 calibration sequences vs GPTQ's 192
- **Domain-robust**: calibration domain shift causes only +0.5–0.6 PPL for AWQ vs +2.3–4.9 PPL for GPTQ (Figure 8)

### TinyChat: On-Device Inference System

AWQ reduces weight memory 4× but W4A16 requires on-the-fly dequantization (INT4 → FP16 for compute). TinyChat converts this theoretical savings into real speedup via:

- **Kernel fusion**: QKV projections fused into single kernel; LayerNorm + quantization scale fused; KV cache pre-allocated and updated in-kernel
- **SIMD-aware weight packing (ARM NEON)**: weights reordered offline so 128-bit SIMD register holds 32 4-bit weights sequenced as w₀,w₁₆,w₁,...,w₃₁ — unpacked with 3 SIMD instructions total vs 3 per-weight scalar instructions
- **On-the-fly dequantization**: fused with matmul kernel, avoids writing dequantized weights to DRAM

## Significance

- **MLSys 2024 Best Paper Award** — recognized as the state-of-the-art PTQ method for LLMs
- **Activation > weight as saliency signal**: the paper proves this empirically and theoretically; activation magnitude is the correct basis for identifying quantization-critical channels
- **Hardware-friendly equivalent transformation**: scaling then inverse-scaling gives same matmul output — no mixed-precision hardware paths needed; scale absorbed into adjacent operators
- **Data efficiency and domain robustness**: 10× fewer calibration sequences; robust to domain shift — better generalist than GPTQ for instruction-tuned and multi-modal models
- **Composability with GPTQ**: orthogonal methods; using both gives best INT2 results
- **HaLViT composability (thesis relevance)**: AWQ measures per-(input-channel) activation statistics for the W→x forward path. For HaLViT's W+Wᵀ shared weight, the "input channels" of W become "output channels" of Wᵀ, and their activation magnitudes in the Wᵀ→x' path are generally different from the W→x path. A naive single-path AWQ calibration will: (1) correctly scale channels salient for W→x; (2) mis-scale channels for Wᵀ→x' because their per-channel importance was not measured. A **joint AWQ** for shared weights would need to measure activation statistics on both forward and transposed paths and find a per-channel scale that minimizes quantization error for BOTH simultaneously — analogous to the joint-Hessian problem in GPTQ and the symmetric-mask problem in SparseGPT. This is a direct, unexplored thesis direction.

## Contradicts / Supports

- **Supports** [[sources/2210.17323]] (GPTQ): confirms the PTQ-at-4-bit paradigm; AWQ and GPTQ are complementary — AWQ corrects GPTQ's single-path limitation by operating on the activation/channel dimension rather than the column-wise weight reconstruction dimension
- **Contradicts** GPTQ's calibration-set strategy: AWQ shows GPTQ overfits calibration domain (+2.3–4.9 PPL shift) while AWQ is domain-robust (+0.5–0.6 PPL). AWQ uses 10× fewer sequences and generalizes better across domains and modalities
- **Relevant to** [[concepts/edge-ai]]: TinyChat enables Llama-2-70B on Jetson Orin and 7B LLMs on Raspberry Pi — directly relevant to edge deployment benchmarks and HaLViT's resource-constrained target platform
- **Relevant to** [[sources/halvit]]: same joint-path calibration problem as GPTQ/SparseGPT for shared weights; joint AWQ is an open thesis direction orthogonal to symmetric-mask (SparseGPT) and joint-Hessian (GPTQ)
