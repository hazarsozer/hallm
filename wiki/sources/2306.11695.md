## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods either require retraining (impractical at billion-scale) or expensive weight reconstruction using second-order Hessian information (SparseGPT). The goal is a pruning method competitive with SparseGPT but requiring neither retraining nor weight updates.

## Method

**Wanda** (Pruning by **W**eights **and** **a**ctivations) prunes weights using a simple product criterion:

```
score(i,j) = |w_ij| × ||x_j||_2
```

For each output neuron i, weights are scored by the product of their magnitude and the L2 norm of the corresponding input activation vector (estimated from 128 calibration sequences, same as SparseGPT). Weights with the smallest scores are set to zero — no gradient computation, no Hessian, no weight update.

**Motivation**: Inspired by LLM.int8()'s observation that some LLM features are "emergent" and have systematically large activation magnitudes. A small weight connected to a high-magnitude activation channel is more important than a large weight connected to a low-magnitude channel — magnitude alone is insufficient for LLMs.

**Supports structured N:M sparsity** (2:4, 4:8) alongside unstructured sparsity, enabling NVIDIA Ampere hardware acceleration.

## Results

| Model | Sparsity | Wanda PPL | Magnitude PPL | SparseGPT PPL |
|-------|----------|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| LLaMA-7B | 50% unstructured | 7.26 | 17.29 | ~6.97 |
| LLaMA-65B | 50% unstructured | matches dense | — | matches dense |
| LLaMA-2-70B | 50% unstructured | matches dense | — | matches dense |

- Wanda outperforms magnitude pruning by large margin at 50% sparsity
- Wanda performs on par with SparseGPT at 50% unstructured sparsity for large models
- SparseGPT slightly better for 2:4 structured sparsity on smaller models (7B); Wanda better on 30B+ for 2:4 and 4:8
- **No weight update, no retraining, no Hessian** — runs in minutes vs hours for SparseGPT
- Calibration: 128 C4 sequences (identical to SparseGPT for fair comparison)

## Significance

Wanda establishes that activation-weighted magnitude is a near-optimal pruning signal for LLMs — achievable without any second-order computation. The insight bridges quantization research (activation importance drives quantization error) and pruning research (activation importance drives weight saliency). At large model scales (65B+), 50% sparsity is free: overparameterization means sparse subnetworks match dense accuracy. Published ICLR 2024.

For HaLViT: Wanda's criterion is immediately applicable to shared W. The activation norm ||x_j|| accumulated across all inputs to shared W automatically captures the importance of column j in both the W→x and Wᵀ→x' pathways — a joint saliency signal without any modification. This is even simpler than SNIP's joint backward pass (no gradient needed — just activation norms from a calibration forward pass).
