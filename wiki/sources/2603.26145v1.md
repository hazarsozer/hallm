## Problem

Few-shot learning (FSL) at the edge: resource constraints prevent deploying large FSL models; most prior FSL work uses ResNet backbones, leaving efficient ViT-based alternatives unexplored. Goal: pre-train a lightweight MobileViT backbone for FSL on edge devices via KD, then deploy on Jetson Orin Nano.

## Method

KD from a large-scale teacher model to MobileViT student, pre-trained for few-shot classification:
- Teacher: large transformer model with strong generalization capacity
- Student: MobileViT (hybrid CNN-ViT, designed for mobile deployment)
- KD focuses on transferring generalization ability (not just task-specific logits) — enabling the small student to learn good few-shot features

Evaluated on MiniImageNet benchmark (1-shot and 5-shot classification). Deployed on **Jetson Orin Nano** with direct power supply measurement (hardware-verified energy consumption).

## Results

| Metric | KD-MobileViT vs ResNet12 baseline |
|--------|----------------------------------|
| 1-shot accuracy | +14% |
| 5-shot accuracy | +6.7% |
| Parameters | −69% |
| FLOPs | −88% |
| Dynamic energy (Jetson Orin Nano) | −37% |
| Inference latency | 2.6 ms |

## Significance

Demonstrates that KD + lightweight ViT (MobileViT) is a strong combination for edge few-shot learning — outperforming ResNet12 at 69% fewer parameters and 88% fewer FLOPs. Hardware-verified results on Jetson Orin Nano (including direct power supply measurement) provide credible edge deployment evidence. The 37% energy reduction is the headline edge AI result.

For HaLViT: this paper is directly analogous to the HaLViT deployment scenario. HaLViT (shared-weight ViT) distilled to a mobile inference target would follow the same KD + edge deployment pipeline. The energy measurement methodology (direct power supply on Jetson Orin Nano) is the gold standard for edge AI evaluation and should be used in the thesis benchmarking chapter. The MobileViT architecture is a natural comparison baseline for HaLViT at similar parameter counts.
