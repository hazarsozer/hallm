## Problem

Most weight-sharing work evaluates a single sharing topology (e.g., ALBERT's all-layer cross-layer sharing) without systematically comparing topologies. It is unclear which sharing mapping (how layers are grouped) or sharing distribution (which group gets unique weights) is best, or how much accuracy is lost under isotropic architectures (ViT, ConvMixer, ConvNeXt) that share a uniform layer dimension — making sharing structurally simpler than BERT's hetero-dimensional stack.

Additionally, initializing shared weights from scratch ignores the pretrained weight information that would otherwise be split across shared layers — a wasted signal.

## Method

### Two-Dimensional Topology Framework

SPIN characterizes every cross-layer sharing scheme along two axes:

**Sharing Mapping** — how layers are grouped into parameter sets:
- **Sequential**: Consecutive groups (1,2,3,4 → {1,2},{3,4} at share rate 2)
- **Strided**: Interleaved groups (layers at offsets 0,2,4,… share one set; 1,3,5,… share another)
- **Pyramid**: Groups grow larger toward the middle; layers at ends are more independent
- **Random**: Random assignment of layers to parameter groups

**Sharing Distribution** — within each group, which instance gets unique weights:
- **Uniform**: All groups have same size
- **Front**: Unique weights concentrated in early layers; later layers share
- **Middle**: Unique weights in middle; ends share
- **Back**: Unique weights in late layers

**Share Rate** (compression metric): `share_rate = L / P` where L = total layers, P = number of unique weight tensors. Share rate 2 = half as many unique weight tensors as layers.

### CKA Similarity Analysis

Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) measures representational similarity between layer outputs. Finding: **middle layers of ConvMixer and ViT have highest cross-layer CKA**, i.e., their internal representations are most redundant. This justifies sharing the middle layers (analogous to Subformer's Sandwich insight, discovered independently).

### Weight Fusion

When adapting a pretrained model for weight sharing, the pretrained weights in layers assigned to the same group must be merged into a single tensor. SPIN tests:
- **Channel Weighted Mean (CWM)**: Per-channel weighted average of the group's pretrained weights
- **Scalar Weighted Mean (SWM)**: Uniform or learned scalar average across the group's weights
- **Standard random init**: No fusion, initialize shared weights from scratch

Weight fusion consistently outperforms from-scratch initialization, recovering 0.5–1.3 percentage points of accuracy.

### Dynamic Transforms

To allow per-layer expressiveness despite sharing, each layer applies a lightweight affine transform f_i(W) = a_i·W + b_i to the shared tensor, with group size G=64 (one scalar pair per G channels). This adds ~7% more parameters but only +0.07% accuracy — marginal benefit, not recommended.

## Results

### ConvMixer (Table 1 — ImageNet, share rate 2)

| Strategy | Params | Top-1 |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Baseline | 20.46M | 75.71% |
| Uniform-Sequential | 11.02M | 73.29% |
| Front-Sequential | 11.02M | **73.31%** |
| Middle-Pyramid | 11.02M | 73.22% |
| Back-Sequential | 11.02M | 72.88% |
| Strided-Uniform | 11.02M | 72.15% |

Front-Sequential slightly best; sharing distribution matters more than mapping.

### Weight Fusion Ablation — ConvMixer (Table 3, share rate 2)

| Init | Params | Top-1 |
|------|--------|-------|
| From scratch (uniform) | 11.02M | 73.29% |
| **Channel Weighted Mean** | **10.84M** | **75.15%** |
| Scalar Weighted Mean | 10.84M | 74.94% |

CWM from pretrained initialization achieves **75.15%** with only −0.56% vs full baseline (20.5M → 10.84M, 2× compression).

### Weight Sharing vs. Model Scaling — ConvMixer (Table 4)

At every iso-parameter budget, weight sharing achieves higher accuracy than simply training a smaller model. A 10.84M weight-shared ConvMixer (75.15%) outperforms a 11M natively-small ConvMixer (~73.5%). The shared middle layers provide more effective capacity than an equivalently-small standalone model.

### ConvNeXt (Table 6, ImageNet, share rate 2)

| Model | Params | Top-1 |
|-------|--------|-------|
| ConvNeXt-18 (baseline) | 22.3M | 78.70% |
| WS-ConvNeXt-18 share rate 2 | 11.5M | **78.07%** |
| ConvNeXt-10 (small equiv.) | ~11M | ~76.4% |

2× parameter reduction with only −0.63% accuracy. Shared ConvNeXt-18 substantially outperforms a native ConvNeXt-10 at the same size.

### DeiT/ViT — MOST RELEVANT TO HaLViT (Table 8, Appendix B)

| Model | Params | Top-1 |
|-------|--------|-------|
| DeiT-S baseline | 22.05M | **80.52%** |
| WS-DeiT-S share rate 2 | 11.41M | 78.61% |
| WS-DeiT-S share rate 2 + Mean fusion | 11.41M | **79.44%** |
| WS-DeiT-S share rate 3 + Mean fusion | 7.87M | **77.11%** |
| WS-DeiT-S share rate 4 + Mean fusion | 5.91M | 75.12% |
| DeiT-Ti baseline | 5.72M | 72.55% |
| WS-DeiT-Ti share rate 2 + SWM | 3.05M | **68.62%** |

Key takeaway: WS-DeiT-S at share rate 2 + weight fusion retains **79.44%** vs 80.52% baseline — **2× fewer params, −1.08%**. At share rate 3, 7.87M params (65% reduction) → 77.11% (−3.41%).

### Component Ablation — ConvMixer (Table 9)

| Shared components | Top-1 |
|-------------------|-------|
| Pointwise Conv only (largest) | 73.29% |
| Depthwise Conv only | 72.11% |
| Both | 72.45% |
| BatchNorm only | **diverged** |

BatchNorm sharing causes training divergence. Only the largest weight tensor (Pointwise Conv) contributes positively. This is a practical safety constraint: normalization layers must never be shared.

## Significance

- **First systematic 2D topology evaluation** for cross-layer sharing in isotropic architectures; establishes best practices empirically rather than heuristically
- **Weight fusion is the critical insight**: pretrained weight fusion (CWM/SWM) recovers most of the accuracy gap vs from-scratch sharing; should be the default initialization strategy for any cross-layer sharing experiment
- **CKA validates middle-layer redundancy** in ViT — middle layers have highest cross-layer representational similarity, supporting sharing strategies that keep first/last layers independent (consistent with Subformer's Sandwich insight)
- **Weight sharing > model scaling**: At any iso-parameter budget, a weight-shared large model outperforms a natively small model — shared weights provide more effective capacity
- **HaLViT composability (thesis relevance)**: SPIN directly evaluates DeiT-S cross-layer sharing in ViT, which is architecturally identical to HaLViT's target. Results show 2× compression at −1.08% accuracy is achievable. Key open question: can HaLViT's existing intra-layer W+Wᵀ sharing be combined with SPIN-style cross-layer sharing to achieve compound compression — and does weight fusion for the shared W+Wᵀ pair require a joint fusion strategy (fusing both W and Wᵀ from pretrained weights simultaneously)?
- **Normalization layer constraint**: BatchNorm sharing diverges. Any HaLViT cross-layer composition must keep normalization layers independent.

## Contradicts / Supports

- **Supports** [[sources/2101.00234]] (Subformer): Middle layers most amenable to sharing; CKA independently confirms the Sandwich insight — first/last layers encode unique representations
- **Supports** [[sources/1909.11942]] (ALBERT): Cross-layer sharing reduces parameters without severe accuracy loss; but ALBERT's all-layer approach is specific to discriminative transformers
- **Extends** [[sources/halvit]]: Intra-layer W+Wᵀ sharing (HaLViT) + cross-layer sharing (SPIN-style) are orthogonal compression axes; SPIN provides the baseline for the cross-layer dimension on ViT
- **Constrains**: BatchNorm/LayerNorm layers must not be shared — any composition with HaLViT must respect this
